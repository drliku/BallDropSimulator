What you are seeing

A point on a circle

The point sits at angle θ on a circle of radius 1. Its coordinates are (cos θ, sin θ): the horizontal leg of the right triangle is cos θ and the vertical leg is sin θ, because the hypotenuse has length 1.

Unrolling the motion

Plot the height of the point against the angle it has turned and you get the sine wave. The dotted lines carry each value straight across to the graph, so one turn of the circle draws exactly one period, 2π.

A quarter turn apart

Cosine reaches its peak at 0°, a quarter turn before sine peaks at 90°. Slide the cosine wave 90° to the right and it lands exactly on the sine wave: sin θ = cos(θ − 90°). Turn on Compare to watch the shift. (The orange arc on the circle turns the cosine length onto the vertical axis so it can be plotted as a height.)

Stretch, squeeze, slide

A scales the height, ω fits more cycles into one turn so the period becomes 2π/ω, and φ slides the wave sideways by −φ/ω. The circle keeps showing the plain functions so you can compare.

Angles are measured counter-clockwise from the positive x-axis. The graph uses the same vertical scale as the circle, so every projection lines up exactly.
